# R8 Financial Analogy Report

- The financial analogy is quantitatively useful inside the frozen game payoff ledger.
- p_wolf behaves like a dynamic risk score, not a real-world probability of default.
- Deception and trust memory are best framed as adversarial manipulation and reputation controls.
- No claim is made that simulated payoffs are externally priced financial returns.

| Component | Analogue | Supported Use | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| p_wolf | dynamic risk score | Useful for explaining how agents update perceived adversarial risk. | Not calibrated to real-world default probabilities or market probabilities. |
| payoff | return or utility payoff | Valid within the game ledger and unchanged manifest. | Not money-valued and not externally priced. |
| downside deviation and VaR/CVaR-like metrics | downside risk and tail risk | Reveals role and strategy tail exposure beyond mean payoff. | No claim that game payoffs follow financial-return distributions. |
| deception | adversarial manipulation and misinformation risk | Useful conceptual bridge to manipulation risk and reputation controls. | Does not model real strategic markets or legal enforcement. |
| speaker memory | reputation-weighted information control | Explains why credibility-weighted signals can dampen manipulation. | Trust scores are synthetic and not learned from human discourse. |
