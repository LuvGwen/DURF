# DURF Werewolf Research Evidence Map

| research chapter | hypothesis | experiment | main result | conclusion | supporting repository files |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline simulation | Wolves dominate when village agents lack information. | Stage 1 ablation with random baseline, suspicion voting, and suspicion update. | Random baseline produced about 7% village wins; suspicion voting and updates improved village wins only modestly. | The simulator creates a meaningful low-information baseline and exposes the village information problem. | `experiment_report.md`, `results/ablation_results.csv` |
| Role actions | Direct information and intervention should reduce wolf advantage. | Seer, witch, and hunter role-action ablations. | Seer action raised village wins to 23%; witch action raised village wins to 49%; hunter action produced 46% village wins in the Stage 1 setup. | Role abilities matter, but their value depends on interaction effects and current role composition. | `experiment_report.md`, `seer_action.py`, `witch_action.py`, `hunter_action.py` |
| Speech and belief | Language-like signals should turn voting into a social inference process. | Stage 2 Bag-of-Words speech, `p_wolf`, herding, and role-prior ablations. | Speech-enabled runs moved village outcomes above the low-information baseline and often above 50%; herding and role prior added smaller gains in exploratory 7-player runs. | Information flow fundamentally changes game dynamics. | `stage2_experiment_report.md`, `results/ablation_results.md`, `results/experiment_results.md` |
| Wolf night strategy | Wolves can improve outcomes by targeting information roles. | Wolf night-kill strategy diagnostics. | `seer_first` was the best exploratory wolf night-kill strategy among the tested policies. | The seer is a critical village information channel. | `stage2_experiment_report.md`, `results/wolf_strategy_results.md`, `wolf_strategy.py` |
| Wolf daytime deception | Wolves can recover advantage by manipulating daytime speech. | Stage 3 deception policy diagnostics. | `false_accuse` initially reached 78% wolf wins, while `false_role_claim` fell to 23% wolf wins. | Deception strategy choice matters; not all deception helps wolves. | `stage3_experiment_report.md`, `wolf_deception.py`, `wolf_deception_experiment.py` |
| Deception credibility costs | Cost-free deception is unrealistic and should be penalized. | Accusation, wrong-accusation, and self-defense cost experiments. | Accusation costs reduced `false_accuse` from 78% to 50%; self-defense costs reduced `deflect_suspicion` and adaptive deception to about 46% wolf wins. | Deception can be powerful, but credibility penalties are necessary to keep it plausible. | `stage3_experiment_report.md`, `deception_credibility.py` |
| Speaker memory | Agents should resist deception better if they remember speaker reliability. | Stage 4 speaker-specific trust memory and sensitivity experiment. | Raising `trust_vote_weight` from 0.00 to 0.40 reduced wolf wins from 47.80% to 36.40% in the sensitivity run. | Speaker-specific memory can help, but trust effects need calibration. | `stage4_experiment_report.md`, `speaker_memory.py`, `trust_update.py` |
| Ten-player stress test | Mechanisms should be tested in a larger, noisier setting. | 10-player single-seed and multi-seed experiments. | Multi-seed results: baseline 43.68% village wins, speech 65.16%, deception 21.60%, credibility cost 41.68%, trust memory 60.32%, trust-weighted herding 48.56%. | The main mechanisms generalize, but larger games require parameter calibration. | `ten_player_experiment_report.md`, `results/ten_player_multi_seed_summary.md` |
| Risk preference | Heterogeneous risk appetite changes collective resilience and payoff. | 10-player risk preference single-seed and multi-seed experiments. | Conservative-majority trust memory had 61.44% village wins; aggressive-majority had 49.04% village wins. | Risk composition affects group-level resilience and payoff distribution. | `risk_preference_experiment_report.md`, `results/ten_player_risk_preference_multi_seed_summary.md` |
| Initial position theory | Edge seats or side structure may guide seer checking. | Fixed-role 10-player seer position experiment. | `highest_p_wolf` and random strategies performed strongly; edge-first improved over default but did not dominate. | Position logic was suggestive but confounded by fixed role-seat assignment. | `seer_position_experiment_report.md`, `results/ten_player_seer_position_multi_seed_summary.md` |
| Randomized role-seat validation | If edge seats are truly informative, edge-first should remain strong after randomization. | Randomized seat-role assignment and multi-seed position experiment. | Edge wolf probability was about 30.23%, inner about 29.85%, expected 30%; edge-first and inner-first were close. | Edge seats are not intrinsically wolf-heavy; position is a weak heuristic. | `seer_position_randomized_roles_report.md`, `results/data_analysis/seer_position_game_level/analysis_report.md` |
| Game-level logging | Aggregate win rates are insufficient to identify the mechanism. | One-row-per-game logging with first-check and seer-survival variables. | Village win was 47.59% when the first check found a wolf and 34.17% otherwise; adjusted OR 1.76, 95% CI 1.65-1.88, p < 0.001. | Early information acquisition is a stronger mechanism than seat category. | `results/data_analysis/seer_position_game_level/analysis_report.md`, `results/ten_player_seer_position_randomized_roles_game_level_raw.csv` |
| Structured sequential search | Search-path structure may improve information acquisition and village outcomes. | 14-strategy structured seer search, 35,000 games. | `alternate_sides` had 44.16% village wins, `right_to_left` 43.88%, `random` 40.52%. | Structured search is promising descriptively, but positive advantages are not fully confirmed statistically. | `results/structured_seer_search/structured_seer_search_experiment_report.md`, `results/data_analysis/structured_seer_search/analysis_report.md` |
| Exploitation vs diversification | Overusing suspicion scores may narrow search and reduce village performance. | Structured search family comparison. | Structured diversification group had 42.66% village wins, while behavioral exploitation had 35.52%. `highest_p_wolf` and `highest_suspicion` were statistically worse than random. | Current evidence favors diversified information search over suspicion-only exploitation. | `results/data_analysis/structured_seer_search/exploitation_vs_diversification.csv`, `results/data_analysis/structured_seer_search/analysis_report.md` |
| Directional asymmetry | The `right_to_left` advantage might be real or might reflect seat-order artifacts. | Structured search analysis and direction-asymmetry audit. | `right_to_left` beat random descriptively but did not survive Holm correction; the asymmetry required follow-up. | Directional effects cannot be interpreted scientifically without engine-symmetry validation. | `results/data_analysis/structured_seer_search/analysis_report.md` |
| Code audit | Numeric labels and deterministic order may influence outcomes. | Seat-order asymmetry code audit. | Potential dependencies include ascending player order, lower-ID tie-breaks, numeric side labels, stable sorting, and speech RNG based on `player_id`. | The engine contains seat-label and order dependencies that must be neutralized before claiming directional effects. | `results/seat_order_symmetry/seat_order_asymmetry_code_audit.md` |
| Mirror validation | If the direction effect is physical, mirrored labels should transform it cleanly. | 30,000-game paired normal/mirrored seat-order symmetry experiment. | The `right_to_left` advantage did not simply reverse; paired outcome agreement was only about 46%-53%. | Mirroring changes downstream trajectories too much for a clean counterfactual; a seat-order-neutral engine is needed. | `results/seat_order_symmetry/seat_order_symmetry_experiment_report.md`, `results/seat_order_symmetry/seat_order_symmetry_pair_summary.csv` |
| Current next stage | Mechanism isolation requires neutralizing seat-label and order effects. | Planned seat-order-neutral engine and retest of directional strategies. | Not yet run. | The project has moved from folklore-like position heuristics to sequential information acquisition under uncertainty. | `results/seat_order_symmetry/seat_order_asymmetry_code_audit.md`, `results/data_analysis/structured_seer_search/analysis_report.md` |
